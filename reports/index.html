Executive summary

Enterprise adoption of agentic AI is diverging into two distinct operational paradigms: horizontal desktop productivity copilots and deeply integrated business workflow agents. Workflow agents command substantial executive interest and capital allocation driven by quantifiable cycle time reductions, direct labor productivity, and 100% automated transaction auditability; however, production scaling is heavily obstructed by unredesigned operating models, legacy API integration friction, strict regulatory scrutiny, and non-human identity security gaps. Conversely, desktop copilot rollouts face widespread post-deployment engagement decay, hidden enablement overheads exceeding license prices, and security halts caused by latent file overexposure and permission hygiene failures. Across both segments, organizations achieving high adoption treat agentic AI as an operating model and systems engineering challenge rather than a generic software rollout, establishing formal evaluation guardrails, non-human identity frameworks, and explicit decision-rights restructuring.

What changed since the last run

Compared to the previous baseline, workflow agent adoption has shifted from speculative experimentation into rigorous operational scrutiny: risk and auditability has solidified as both a major driver (when enabling 100% transaction surveillance) and a rigid inhibitor (due to lack of reversible execution controls and machine IAM). Regulatory compliance friction has escalated from abstract policy tracking into active enforcement under consumer protection statutes (CFPB). In the desktop copilot segment, initial enthusiasm for summarization has been tempered by empirical evidence of post-launch engagement decays (72% struggling with habit integration) and hidden enablement costs ($6.9M in training vs. $5.8M in software licenses) alongside widespread pauses triggered by latent file overexposure.
