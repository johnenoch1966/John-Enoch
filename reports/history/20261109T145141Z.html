How scores are calculated

Every ranking on this page is computed from the evidence by a fixed formula (weights in adoption_agent/scoring.yaml). The AI writes the labels and explanations; it does not set the order.

1. Each claim gets a weight

weight = source quality × recency (× mapping confidence for drivers/blockers)

Source typeWeight
regulator1.0

other0.5
vendor-affiliated (any type)max 0.5

Recency: &le;1 yr → 1.0; &le;2 yr → 0.8; &le;3 yr → 0.6; &gt;3 yr → 0.4.

2. Weights are combined into scores (0–100)

Weighted evidence = sum over distinct sources of each source's strongest claim, so one report counts once.

Drivers & blockers: weighted evidence of the root-cause group, as a % of the strongest group in the same segment or industry. Labels: strong ≥ 66, moderate ≥ 33, else weak.

Use cases: score = 100 × evidence0.5 × (0.4 + 0.6 × positivity), where evidence is weighted evidence as a share of the best-covered use case in the segment and positivity is the drivers' share of weighted evidence. The square root dampens heavily covered topics; well-evidenced use cases with mostly positive evidence rank highest.

Hover any score for its breakdown. The "AI rating" (1–5) is the analyst's qualitative view, shown for context only.

Evidence & method

A multi-agent ADK pipeline: a scoping agent selects the top 50 deployed use cases; parallel researchers (per use-case cluster and per industry) read public reports and record cited claims; a semantic-bias analyst maps each claim to a root cause and industry; segment and industry analysts rank findings; a critic validates every citation. Evidence accumulates across runs.

Privacy: personal data (emails, phone numbers, payment cards, IBANs, national ID numbers, IP addresses, credentials in URLs) is masked before content reaches the model, before evidence is stored and before anything is logged. No PII found in the current evidence base.
